# Figure Index

### Figure 1 Tree Plot of subspecies present in East Africa.
Tree plot considering subspecies present in East Africa, the tree was made by clustering principal component analysis results, which was performed using ~6,000,000 WGS SNPs. Labels are coloured according to the subspecies. Newly sequenced Rwenzori mountains samples are represented with roman number labels I – VII and RW.

### Figure 2 Pairwise FST comparison of East African monticola and scutellata populations. 
Blue and green colour codes were used to represent regions of increased genetic differentiation on chromosome 7 and chromosome 9 respectively. a) Genome-Wide FST comparison of highland (n = 38) and lowland (n = 25) honey bees the black line is an average of sliding windows of 1000 non overlapping SNPs. b) and c) Zoom-in of chromosome 7 and chromosome 9 with the relative linkage disequilibrium heatmaps. Heatmaps were generated by calculating one r^2 value every 50 SNPs using the LDHeatmap R package (Shin, et al., 2006). 

### Figure 3 Inversion status of Mau, Mt. Kenya and Rwenzori mountain systems. 
Tree plots consider only r7 (left) and r9 (right) genomic regions from Kenyan and Ugandan populations. Uganda population sampled on the Rwenzori mountains cluster on its own in both cases. This suggests a different haplotype for the two inverted regions. This new haplotype also causes heterozygous individuals in Uganda to not cluster with heterozygous individuals sampled in Kenya.

### Figure 4 Venn Diagram of shared common genes. 
Selection scan XP-nSl algorithm on Mau (blue), Mt. Kenya (Green) and Rwenzori (Black) populations in East Africa revealed a different number of genes involved in highland adaptation. The plot highlights unique genes to single populations as well as shared genes between them. 

### Figure 5 Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs) derived from the comparison of Rwenzori mountains highland versus lowland samples.
Highlighted are the common genes identified in both, the selection scan analysis and the DEGs analysis. The blue scale represents downregulated genes in the reference population (savannah lowland honey bees) and the orange scale represents the upregulated genes in the reference population. Black dots show significantly differently expressed genes (p < 0.05) but with insufficient change in expression given the chosen threshold of 0.7.
